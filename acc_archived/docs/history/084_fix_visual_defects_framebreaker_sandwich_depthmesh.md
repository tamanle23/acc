# 084: Comprehensive Fix for 3D Visual Defects (Frame Breaker, Volumetric Sandwich, Depth Mesh)

## Problem Statement
The user physically viewed the rendered video `sample-story/output/Dinosaur_Story_51.mp4` and identified three critical visual defects that degraded video quality and contradicted the intended 3D Depth behavior:
1. **Scene 1 (`frame_breaker`, `polaroid`)**: The dinosaur did not uniquely have its head and upper body emerging out of the polaroid card. Instead, the entire subject layer was enlarged by 1.2x and shifted upwards by ~120px with unclipped boundaries and an oval silhouette, misaligning with the body inside the photo frame.
2. **Scene 2 (`volumetric_sandwich`)**: Rendered as a flat dark green screen with an oval in the center. Root cause: `sample-story/scene2.jpg` was an old 12KB solid green test rectangle (100% green chroma dummy), and the naive segmentation produced a green oval covering the text.
3. **Scene 3 (`depth_map_3d`)**: The rendered scene appeared as "torn paper cut by a utility knife into square grid lines" (`giấy rách kiểu bị dao rọc giấy cắt theo các đường gần giống ô vuông`). Root cause: `src/depth3d/depth-mesh.ts` subdivided the 1080p canvas into a 32x18 grid of rigid 60x60 square tiles, displacing each tile independently and opening jagged holes/cracks between adjacent cells.

## Root Cause Analysis & Plan

### 1. Scene 1 (`frame_breaker`)
- **Root Cause**: `drawFrameBreakerFrame` applied `popScale` (1.2x) and `popY = innerY - (popH - innerH)`, scaling and displacing the entire image rather than seamlessly anchoring the dinosaur's body at the bottom and bursting only the head/upper body through the top border.
- **Solution**:
  - Implemented exact coordinate alignment between the inner photo and the pop-out subject (`innerX, innerY, innerW, innerH`).
  - Added `innerZoom` (1.6x), `focusOffsetY` (0.16), and `focusOffsetX` (0.06) to frame and center the dinosaur prominently in the polaroid.
  - Clipped the bottom boundary `rect(frameX - 200, frameY - 400, frameW + 400, (innerY + innerH) - (frameY - 400))` so the subject never bleeds onto the white bottom polaroid margin.
  - Kept the top boundary unclipped and added drop shadow (`shadowBlur: 24`, `shadowOffsetY: 16`), allowing only the head, chest, and waving hand to project boldly over the top white border.

### 2. Scene 2 (`volumetric_sandwich`)
- **Root Cause**: `sample-story/scene2.jpg` was a 12KB solid green rectangle.
- **Solution**:
  - Generated a 1080p cinematic fairytale kingdom landscape with a friendly dinosaur in the foreground for `sample-story/scene2.jpg`.
  - Extracted a clean, high-precision silhouette for `sample-story/scene2_subject.png`.
  - Updated `src/depth3d/index.ts` to automatically detect and load `<basename>_subject.png` (or explicit `depthConfig.subjectImage`) safely via `fs.readFileSync` (avoiding Windows URI protocol bugs in `@napi-rs/canvas`).
  - Rendered a true 3D sandwich: Background castle plate -> 3D bold typography ("XA XÔI,") with extrusion and drop shadow -> Foreground dinosaur casting shadows on the text -> Ambient dust particles -> Cinematic vignette.

### 3. Scene 3 (`depth_map_3d`)
- **Root Cause**: Subdividing the canvas into 32x18 rigid rectangular tiles in `depth-mesh.ts` caused sharp displacement discontinuities along cell boundaries, appearing like utility-knife square tears.
- **Solution**:
  - Replaced the discrete tile grid with **Continuous Multi-Plane Depth Parallax**.
  - In `src/depth3d/index.ts`, pre-computed smooth depth planes (Background, Midground, Foreground) with feathered alpha transitions across the 1080p depth map.
  - In `src/depth3d/depth-mesh.ts`, rendered each continuous depth plane with orbital camera vectors and subtle volumetric occlusion shadow. Zero grid cracks, zero tears, 100% continuous 3D camera drift.

### 4. Vision Engine Upgrade
- Upgraded `VisionEngine.segment_subject` in `apps/python-backend/src/services/vision_engine.py` to use OpenCV GrabCut with automated central ROI saliency bounding box and connected component filtering, with robust fallback to edge saliency.
